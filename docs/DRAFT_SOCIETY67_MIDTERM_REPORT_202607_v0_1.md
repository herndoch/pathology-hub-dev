### 4. Current Status / Progress

The project is on track educationally and has grown beyond the original cytopathology demo into a broader **Pathology Knowledge Hub** that residents can actually browse and ask questions of.

**What we set out to do**  
Help trainees find the right teaching moment in long lectures and reference materials—at the time of need (e.g. at the microscope)—instead of scrubbing unsearchable video files or relying only on memory from “just-in-case” lectures.

**What we have now (public / board-oriented layer)**  
- A working Hub website where residents can **browse by organ system and diagnosis** and get **cited answers** drawn from textbooks, journals, PathologyOutlines, WHO material, and lectures.  
- **Board-relevant organization:** content is mapped to disease categories aligned with how trainees study for boards (American Board of Pathology–style topic structure, together with WHO disease names)—so search is not only keyword matching, but navigable like a study outline.  
- **Lecture search that jumps to the moment:** we are systematically adding high-yield board-review lectures (including PathCast “PATHBOARDS” talks). Residents can search a topic and open the video at the relevant timestamp.  
- Early PathCast board-review lectures (e.g. GU; hematopathology/molecular boards content) are already findable this way.

**How this expands the original proposal**  
The grant proposed a cytopathology-focused proof-of-concept and then ingesting UCSF’s own teaching library. Mid-term, the **public proof-of-concept is richer than planned** (multi-source Hub + diagnosis browse + board-lecture timestamps + teaching-oriented chat interface). That strengthens resident and board relevance now, while we continue the funded Phase 1 work: bringing in **UCSF departmental teaching materials** (didactics only; no patient tumor boards) and running the planned trainee surveys.

**Still ahead (core of the funded year)**  
Bulk ingest of UCSF’s internal lecture/slide teaching archive, and the pre/post surveys of resident confidence and time-to-answer.

### 5. Challenges or obstacles?

- **Accessing some online lectures** is slowed by platform restrictions (e.g. age gates); we work around this with a supervised upload workflow and skip titles that cannot be processed cleanly.  
- **Turning a full lecture into useful study “bites”** means we keep the clearest, topic-matched segments for search—not every spoken sentence. That improves finding the right teaching point; it is not a substitute for watching the whole talk.  
- **UCSF internal content** needs careful collection and privacy-safe handling (teaching materials only). That coordination takes longer than public board-review lectures.  
- **Formal resident surveys** still need to be scheduled around the residency calendar.

### 6. Future Goals and Plan for Project Completion

1. Ingest the UCSF Pathology teaching library (videos/slides; didactic content only).  
2. Continue adding PathCast / board-review lectures so the Hub stays useful for exam-oriented study while institutional content comes online.  
3. Complete pre- and post-use surveys with UCSF trainees (confidence, speed of finding answers, use of departmental teaching videos).  
4. Prepare a manuscript for *Academic Pathology* and/or an informatics education abstract, with Society of ’67 acknowledgment.  
5. Longer-term (future funding, not required to finish this grant): a more “tutor-like” system that reasons across sources—not only search.
